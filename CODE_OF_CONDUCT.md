# Contributor Covenant 3.0 Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We are committed to making the lit-shell.js community welcoming, safe, and
equitable for everyone who participates in good faith.

Every participant deserves dignity and respect, regardless of race, ethnicity,
caste, color, age, physical characteristics, neurodiversity, disability, sex,
gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, language,
philosophy, religion, national or social origin, economic position, education,
experience level, or other personal status.

## Encouraged behavior

Our words can be understood differently across cultures, backgrounds, and
native languages. With that in mind, community members are expected to:

- respect the project's purpose and the people contributing to it;
- communicate kindly, honestly, and with reasonable patience;
- welcome different technical viewpoints and lived experiences;
- give specific, constructive feedback about work rather than people;
- receive constructive feedback with care;
- take responsibility for mistakes and their effects;
- make a good-faith effort to repair harm; and
- help sustain a community where others can participate safely.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior, including credible threats or encouragement of it, is
not acceptable:

- harassment, stalking, or continuing unwanted attention after a clear request
  to stop;
- insults, humiliation, intimidation, or attacks directed at a person or group;
- discrimination or stereotyping based on identity, background, or personal
  traits;
- sexualized language, imagery, conduct, or unwelcome sexual attention;
- threats of violence or encouragement of harm, self-harm, or harassment;
- sharing or threatening to share private information without permission;
- intentionally exposing a person's identity or sensitive circumstances;
- sustained disruption of project spaces, moderation, or collaboration;
- deliberate misinformation, impersonation, spam, or other bad-faith
  participation;
- retaliation against someone who reports a concern or assists an
  investigation; and
- other conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a
  professional open-source community.

Differences of opinion and ordinary technical conflict are not automatically
Code of Conduct violations. Participants should still handle disagreement in
line with the encouraged behavior above.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to all project spaces, including repositories,
issues, pull requests, reviews, release channels, and project-related events.
It also applies when someone officially represents lit-shell.js, such as by
using a project account or acting as an appointed project representative.

## Reporting a concern

Report possible violations privately to the project maintainers through a
private contact method published on the [repository owner's GitHub
profile](https://github.com/lsadehaan). If no private maintainer channel is
available and the conduct occurred on GitHub, use GitHub's content-reporting
tools. Do not put personal, identifying, or sensitive details in a public
issue.

Include the relevant dates, locations, context, supporting material, and any
immediate safety concern when practical. A person may report conduct they
experienced or witnessed.

Maintainers will:

- take reports seriously and review them as promptly and fairly as possible;
- limit report details to the people needed to investigate and respond;
- disclose conflicts of interest and recuse themselves when appropriate;
- prioritize the safety and privacy of reporters and affected people; and
- communicate an outcome when doing so is safe and appropriate.

Knowingly false reports and retaliation are violations. A report that cannot
be substantiated is not, by itself, a false report.

## Addressing and repairing harm

Responses should be proportionate to impact, context, history, safety, and the
willingness and ability to repair harm. Maintainers may use one or more of the
following actions:

### 1. Guidance or correction

For lower-impact conduct, a maintainer may privately explain the problem,
request an edit or apology, and clarify expected future behavior.

### 2. Formal warning or participation limits

For a clear violation or repeated lower-impact conduct, a maintainer may issue
a written warning and temporarily limit contact, reviews, posting, or other
forms of participation. The conditions and consequences of another violation
should be stated clearly.

### 3. Temporary removal

For serious or sustained violations, access to some or all community spaces may
be removed for a defined period. Any conditions for returning should be
communicated privately when appropriate.

### 4. Permanent removal

For severe harm, credible safety risks, retaliation, or a persistent pattern of
violations, access to project spaces may be removed permanently.

Possible repair can include correcting the public record, removing harmful
content, acknowledging impact, apologizing without demanding forgiveness,
changing behavior, or restoring work and credit. No affected person is
required to participate in direct reconciliation. Safety takes priority over
restoration, and some harm may make restoration impossible.

This enforcement ladder guides, but does not replace, maintainer judgment.
Actions may be escalated immediately when needed to protect the community.
